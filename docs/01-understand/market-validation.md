# Market validation plan

**Status** Customer validation pending. This milestone produced a research protocol and a desk review. Interview findings, observational data, baseline timings and customer feedback are unavailable.

## Recruitment and method

Recruit six Surat wholesale businesses for 25–35 minute sessions, deliberately including WhatsApp chat-only and catalog users, print or PDF users, and spreadsheet users. Include businesses with different assortment sizes and rates of color or price changes. Observe a sales operator when someone other than the owner answers buyers. Recruit four buyers across garment manufacturing and wholesale procurement. Treat findings as directional for these participants.

Start with unaided walkthroughs of recent activity. With permission, ask to see redacted lists or on-screen examples. Record participant codes, evidence type, workflow, count, elapsed time and counterexamples. Avoid copying chats, customer names, numbers, rates identified as confidential, or order records into the repository. After the baseline, run a short mock-catalog task using the supplied sample fabric with no live buyer data.

## Surat wholesaler discussion guide

1. Walk me through the last request you received for a new fabric. What came in, what did you send and what happened next?
2. Show how you would find a fabric photo and its quality, design code, GSM, width or panna and available colors today. Where do you look first?
3. Think of the last rate or color availability change. Who updated it and how did sales staff and buyers find out?
4. For the last ten inquiries you can review safely, which questions repeated, how often, and how much work did they take to answer?
5. When two customers get different rates for the same design, what changes between the deals? Which rates would you publish on a link anyone can forward?
6. How do you describe MOQ in meters, lumps, rolls or another unit? Does it change by quality or color? Show a recent example with sensitive values hidden.
7. Walk through one recent physical sample and one quotation request. What information arrived up front, what did you ask for, and who followed up?
8. When did a buyer last receive an old PDF, photo, price or availability answer? How did you discover and correct it? If this rarely happens, why?
9. Which existing catalog, WhatsApp feature, spreadsheet or marketplace already works well? What would you keep even if you had another catalog tool?
10. Use this example to make a listing and share it. Where would this fit into your day? Who would make the next stock change and what would stop them?

With buyers, ask them to find a known design and an unfamiliar fabric meeting a small set of specs, explain what availability and MOQ mean to them, then attempt one sample or quote request. Ask what would make them leave the catalog and contact the seller directly.

## Preliminary decision rules

- Treat repetitive work as promising if at least four of six wholesalers each show two or more avoidable fact-check or clarification contacts among their last ten comparable inquiries, and at least three identify a person who would maintain pricing and availability.
- Treat self-service discovery as promising if at least three of four buyers can shortlist the correct fabric unaided in the mock task and explain the next step.
- Revisit price exposure if at least three wholesalers decline any public numeric rates. Revisit the catalog-first approach if existing tools solve the same tasks with comparable effort or stale stock originates upstream with no owner.
- Record negatives, denominator, dates, scope and supporting redacted notes. Validate the proposed pilot targets separately using the [success metrics](success-metrics.md).

Desk research shows that product catalogs and marketplace quote prompts already exist. It does not establish local workflow prevalence or a customer need for FabricRelay. See [public sources and alternatives](competitor-analysis.md).
